# Case 001 — Security Onion so-test BitTorrent Alert Investigation

**Exercise Type:** Known Synthetic/Validation Exercise

1. **Scenario:** Through running the "so-test" alert on Security Onion, I generated a number of alerts. The one I investigated was "ET P2P BitTorrent Peer Sync".
2. **Environment:** The environment is a Security Onion machine accessed through an Ubuntu Linux machine.
3. **Investigation Method:** I looked a bit into BitTorrent since I didn't fully understand its purpose yet. Based on my research, I concluded it was unusual to detect on a corporate network. I hypothesized this was an attempt at data exfiltration from an unauthorized third party.
4. **Evidence:** I looked into the source and destination IP addresses of the alert. The source was from an internal IP address, and the destination led to Belem, Brazil. I thought this might indicate a communication with someone not related to the company.
5. **Timeline:** Alerts like the indicated one started at 10:48:20 and ended at 10:59:02.
6. **ATT&CK Mapping:** I hypothesized this attack was an "Exfiltration through Alternate Protocol", as BitTorrent is not a protocol typically used by servers to communicate.
7. **Manual Findings:** Looking into the destination IP address, I learned it came from a legitimate Brazilian digital infrastructure company called V Tal. I theorized the alert may have been a false positive triggered by a corporate partner attempting to access resources. However, I found it strange they would use BitTorrent for such a purpose. I theorized this may have been attempted exfiltration either by a rogue V Tal employee or by a third party using them as a proxy. In addition to the above mapping, this would classify the attack as a "Trusted Relationship" method.
8. **AI-Assisted Findings:** The AI found a few things I had overlooked, but there were two that stuck out. First of all, it determined based on packet capture there was little activity from BitTorrent, indicating the attempted exfiltration was unsuccessful. Therefore, it classified the alert as an "emerging threat", rather than an "indicator of compromise". Second, it noted the Brazilian destination IP as normal; BitTorrent servers are located around the world. This would mean the attack may have not even originated from Brazil. In addition, my initial hypothesis about the "Trusted Relationship" ATT&CK method was also most likely incorrect.
9. **Where AI Was Wrong:** The AI only had the context of the singular alert. After looking at alerts similar to the selected one, I noticed a number of destination IPs located around the globe. This fell in line with the earlier statement about destination IPs related to BitTorrent. Therefore, I determined all the alerts were likely part of the same attack, meaning this was not an attempt at connection from V Tal.
10. **Final Analyst Judgment:** I believe the test traffic represented a true positive for an attempt at data exfiltration by a malicious third party. I initially hypothesized this based on unexpected BitTorrent activity. However, I can not know for sure whether exfiltration occurred or who was responsible. The IP address tied to a foreign company may indicate an attack, but it alone is not sufficient evidence. In addition, I have no way of knowing the company's external connections to confirm or deny theories of relationship abuse. I would need to analyze data related to the exact nature of the traffic, which I do not have access to. In a production environment, this would demand further investigation before determining intent.
11. **Lessons Learned:** I learned two things. The first, it is important to fully understand whatever activity is detected on the network. The second, it is often necessary to look at other alerts to gain the full context behind an alert's nature.
